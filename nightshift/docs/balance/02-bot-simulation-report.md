Headless harness for `nightshift/src/sim.ts`, driven by statistical bots, 300 rounds per configuration,
4 player counts x 3 skill profiles x 2 policies = 24 configurations = 7,200 rounds per experiment
(about 16 s of wall time per experiment). Game source was not modified; everything lives in

Files:

- `build-tuned/` same compiled sim with every balance literal routed through `tuning.js` (`T.*`); with default values it reproduces the shipped sim bit-for-bit (verified: identical results on identical seeds). Generated by `make-tuned.js`.

## 1. How the harness models play

- `Sim` is constructed with the two callbacks, players added, `start()`, then `tick(1/30)` and `handle()` exactly as a host would.
- Movement: bots own their position (as clients do). They BFS over the tile grid (`isSolid`, 4-connected, so no corner clipping with the 0.28 radius) and walk tile-centre to tile-centre at 4.6 tiles/s, sending `{t:'pos'}` every tick. They interact from a walkable tile within 1.4 tiles of the object (client limit 1.5, server limit 1.6). Warps (`warp` message) resync the bot and freeze it for 0.75 s (server ignores pos for 0.7 s).
- Target choice: every candidate gets `value + 20 (if it advances an open objective) - 1.2 x path length - 15 (if a teammate is already heading there) +- 2 jitter`. Base values: pillow 100 (when carrying the mouse), mouse 30, cabinet 25, tp 20, zoomies 20, window 18, knockable 15, rug 15, groom 14 (teammate within 6 tiles, 14 if the groom objective is open), vent 12 (sit 5-10 s, or until the vent objective is done), shove 2 (only when nothing else is available). Failed `use` calls (no `mgstart`/no state change) penalise that target for 4 s. Think time 0.3-1.0 s after each minigame.
- Minigames resolve statistically with the duration from the spec (or earlier on success):

| profile | swipe ok | pick per pin | mash quality | zoomies quality | stare | rhythm | balance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| casual | 60% | 70% | U(0.40,1.00) -> ok 83% | U(0.50,0.95) | 75% | 65% | 60% |
| skilled | 90% | 92% | U(0.65,1.00) -> ok 100% | U(0.80,1.00) | 92% | 90% | 90% |
| clumsy | 35% | 45% | U(0.20,0.85) -> ok 54% | U(0.30,0.75) | 40% | 35% | 35% |

  Mash success is `quality >= 0.5` (that is what `Mash.finish()` produces). Pick is a random walk over pins (+1/-1, 0.5-1.1 s per attempt, 14 s limit; 4 pins unless a teammate happens to stand within 2.5 tiles). Swipe has a 1-6% "walked away" timeout. A groom needs rhythm AND balance to succeed (casual 39%, skilled 81%, clumsy 12%).
- Policies: `reckless` never stops. `caution` stops acting when suspicion > 60, walks to the nearest of six safe tiles (room corners the patrol never approaches) and stands still until suspicion < 35.
- Caveat: bots act continuously (45-50% of the round inside minigames, 25-35% walking). Human players chat, hesitate and mis-path, so measured noise rates, bust rates and hiding fractions are upper bounds for real groups. Bots do not coordinate the cabinet helper, so the 2-pin pick is rare.

Two map facts the bots exposed, worth knowing when reading the numbers:
- The vent (9.5,7.5) and the cat bed (1.5,7.5) are exactly 4.0 tiles from the nearest patrol point (5.5,7.5); spray range is 3.2. Neither can ever be sprayed. The vent is therefore a zero-noise, zero-risk 2 chaos/s faucet.
- The cabinet stand tile (19.5,3.5) is 3.16 from patrol point (18.5,6.5), the rug/tea-cup tile (6.5,4.5) is 3.16 from (5.5,7.5), and the pillow approach tile (29.5,4.5) is 2.0 from (29.5,6.5): all inside spray range with line of sight (furniture does not block LOS). The zoomies tile (2.5,11.5) is 3.0 from (5.5,11.5).

## 4. Answers

### Does the game get easier or harder with more players, and by how much?

Much harder per player; barely "easier" in team total, and only for careful teams.

- Casual, careful: chaos per player 742 -> 447 -> 306 -> 238 for 1/2/3/4 players (-68% at 4p). Team total 742 -> 894 -> 919 -> 953: four players score 28% more than one.
- Casual, reckless: team total 750 -> 608 -> 366 -> 348 and bust rate 1% -> 61% -> 98% -> 99%. A four-cat reckless team scores less than half a solo cat.
- Hiding fraction for careful casual teams: 7% -> 32% -> 46% -> 51%. Patrol time per round: 21 s -> 94 s -> 134 s -> 148 s (of 240).
- Skilled teams are hit the same way: careful 911 -> 649 -> 468 -> 382 per player; reckless skilled bust 0% / 3% / 45% / 85%.

Mechanism: suspicion decay is a fixed 1.2/s team-wide budget (288 points per round) while noise is per player. An active casual bot emits about 0.55 noise/s (0.65 reckless at 1p: 281 noise in 240 s with 8% stunned/idle). So 1 player is comfortably under the decay, 2 players sit at break-even, and a team of N must be idle roughly (N*0.55 - 1.2)/(N*0.55) of the time: 27% at 3p, 45% at 4p, which is exactly what the caution bots measured (46% / 51%) once hysteresis is added. Nothing else in the sim scales with N except the cabinet helper (2 pins) and the groom objective.

### Is the suspicion meter calibrated so that 4 reckless bust, 4 careful don't, and 1 player can complete objectives?

Literally yes, and that is the problem:
- 4 reckless casual players bust 99% of rounds (mean bust time 97 s); 4 careful casual players bust 2.7%; a solo casual player completes all 3 objectives 100% of the time (by 51 s on average), a solo clumsy player 93-95%.
- But 4 careful players only avoid the bust by standing still 51% of the round and finish with 238 chaos each. 2 reckless players are a coin flip (61% bust, 65% clumsy). A solo reckless player cannot bust (1.3%) and a solo skilled player never even triggers a patrol (4%): at 1p the meter is decoration, at 3-4p it is the whole game. The meter is calibrated to team size, not to behaviour.

### Which actions dominate chaos, and which are never worth it?

Dominant sources (share of gross positive chaos):
- Solo casual: successful knocks 18%, pillow gift 16%, objective bonuses 16%, cabinet 11%, zoomies 9%, rug 7%. Four big-ticket items (pillow 40, cabinet 30, objective 25/50) carry the score.
- 4p casual careful: knocks 15%, pillow 12%, objectives 12%, zoomies 10%, groom 9%, cabinet 9%, rug 8%, vent 7%.
- 4p skilled: grooming becomes the top source (308 chaos per round reckless, 283 careful; 25 chaos for 0 noise with an 81% success rate) followed by knocks (259-289) and the vent (130-191). Skilled 4p teams sit on the vent 16-23 times per round and shove each other off it 6-10 times.

Noise efficiency (casual): groom 12 chaos per noise, stare 9.8, rug 6.4, tp 4.8, pick 3.7, pillow 3.3, zoomies 2.4, swipe 1.0 (clumsy swipe 0.63). The vent and a successful stare/groom are free.

Never worth it:
- Knocking things over for casual or clumsy players. A smash is 6 chaos for 20 noise (3.3x the noise of a success for 40% of the reward). Smashes alone are 43% of all noise a casual team makes (119 of 281 per solo round, 134 of 326 at 4p careful) and 52% for clumsy players. Knockables are the most common interactable (7 of 15) and bots still find them, so this gamble shapes the whole suspicion curve.
- The shove: 0 chaos, +6 dominance for the shover, 1.2 s stun for the victim, no team benefit. Pure griefing (and bots only do it when nothing else is available; real players will do it more).
- Grooming for low-skill pairs: 12% success, otherwise 8 or 3 chaos and a 3-noise bap.
- Zoomies as a noise source: 2 + 10q noise for 30q chaos is the second-worst ratio after smashing, although it is time-efficient and risk-free (always "succeeds").

### Which objectives are too easy or impossible in practice?

None is impossible. Nearly all are trivial:
- knock3, cabinet, mouse, tp, vent and hairball complete in 99-100% of rounds at every N for casual and skilled players. The whole board is cleared by 22-30 s (skilled, 3-4p) to 39-51 s (casual), leaving 80% of a 240 s round with nothing on the board.
- Hardest: lick (41% for clumsy 2p, both halves must succeed), stare (54% clumsy 4p reckless, 69% 3p), zoomies2 (52% clumsy 4p reckless; it needs q >= 0.5 twice), cabinet for clumsy (87% at 4p reckless; the 4-pin pick at 45% per pin usually times out).
- knock3 (7 knockables, 50 s cooldown), vent (20 s of a zero-risk action), tp and mouse are the "too easy" ones; they are one trip each.

Lowering the smash noise is the wrong lever: it silences the solo game (patrol 58% -> 1%) and removes the 2-player bust entirely. ### Proposed changes (exact)

All in `src/sim.ts`:

1. Suspicion decay scales with player count. In `tick()`:
   `this.suspicion = clamp(this.suspicion - 1.2 * dt, 0, 100);`
   becomes
   `this.suspicion = clamp(this.suspicion - (1.2 + 0.2 * (this.players.size - 1)) * dt, 0, 100);`
   i.e. 1.2 / 1.4 / 1.6 / 1.8 per second for 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 players. (0.3 per extra player is the upper bound: beyond it 4 reckless casual players stop busting.)

2. Objective targets scale with player count, in `pickObjectives(playerCount)`:
   - `knock3`: `target: 3` -> `target: 2 + playerCount` (3/4/5/6; there are 7 knockables on a 50 s cooldown)
   - `zoomies2`: `target: 2` -> `target: 2 + Math.floor((playerCount - 1) / 2)` (2/2/3/3)
   - `vent`: `target: 20` -> `target: 15 + 5 * playerCount` (20/25/30/35 s)
   - `lick`: `target: 1` -> `target: playerCount >= 3 ? 2 : 1`
   - board size: `return pool.slice(0, 3)` -> `return pool.slice(0, playerCount >= 3 ? 4 : 3)`
   - and build the `text` strings from the numbers (`Knock ${n} things off counters`, etc.).

3. Optional, independent of calibration: smash reward `this.score(p, 6, 1)` -> `this.score(p, 10, 1)` in the `swipe` failure branch. Keep the 20 noise. This lifts the knock gamble from 1.0 to about 1.3 chaos per noise for casual players without touching any bust/hide number.

Not recommended: lowering smash noise, changing ROUND_SECONDS, changing the -15 spray.
